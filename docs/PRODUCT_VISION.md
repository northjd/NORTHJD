# Product Vision

## The problem

Senior consultants have a genuine information problem disguised as a time problem.
They need to know what moved in their industries today, understand markets deeply
enough to hold their own with a CFO, and walk into a client conversation with a
specific, well-grounded question. The tools they have optimise for none of this: news
apps optimise for volume and attention, aggregators for coverage, general chatbots for
fluency without provenance. The default fallback — passive scrolling — consumes exactly
the attention that could have built durable market competence.

## The product

**Market Intelligence OS** is a personal business-intelligence, market-intelligence,
industry-learning, research, meeting-preparation and AI-thought-partner system in one
coherent platform. It replaces passive consumption with a finite, evidence-grounded
daily practice that compounds into real market depth.

It is explicitly **not** another news app, not an RSS reader, and not a professionally
styled Instagram.

## Three connected intelligence layers

| Layer | Answers | Time horizon |
|---|---|---|
| **Market Pulse** | What is new? What changed? What gains or loses momentum? What is urgent? What was corrected? What is corroborated? | days |
| **Market Model** | How does this industry work? Value chain, business models, profit pools, KPIs, capabilities, operating models, technology, regulation | years |
| **Conversation & Application** | What does this mean for a client? Which hypothesis? Which question? Which counter-position? What to learn next? | the next meeting |

The layers are linked in the data model, not just in the UI: a Market Pulse event
points at the Market Model elements it touches, and that link is what produces
insights, client implications, conversation starters, hypotheses, learning
recommendations and meeting briefs.

## The knowledge flywheel

```
Discover → Understand → Connect → Retain → Apply → Reflect → Improve
```

Retention uses spaced resurfacing, short reflection prompts, "what you learned this
week" and "what changed since you learned this" — never manipulative streaks.
Reflection is explicit: *I already knew this · This was new to me · I need more
context · This changed my view · I used this in a conversation · Not relevant · Save
for later · Challenge this conclusion.* Those signals feed selection, depth and
learning paths.

## What makes it defensible

Not source access — the sources are largely public. The defensible layer is the
intelligence built on top: event consolidation across documents, claim-level evidence,
first-party vs. independent perspective, announcement-vs-implementation maturity,
personal knowledge state, and the translation of all of it into conversation-ready
output.

## Users

Primary: strategy and transformation consultants, client account leads, business
development leads, industry leads, functional experts, senior managers, MDs, partners.
Secondary: corporate strategy teams, transformation offices, innovation teams, business
unit leaders, senior executives.

They share a profile: very little time, high need for currency, relevance, strategic
framing, defensible sources, industry fundamentals and concrete conversation
preparation — with a controlled, finite information experience, variable answer depth,
and a professional experience on both mobile and desktop.

## Ten questions answered daily

1. What genuinely matters today?
2. What changed since my last visit?
3. Which developments touch my industries, companies, technologies, accounts and
   learning goals?
4. Why is this strategically relevant?
5. Which statement is defensible and which is marketing?
6. How does this fit a larger pattern?
7. What must I understand about the underlying industry?
8. What could this mean for a client or market participant?
9. Which high-quality question can I ask about it?
10. Where are the information gaps and uncertainties?
