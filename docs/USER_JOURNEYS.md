# User Journeys

Seven journeys, each with what actually happens in the built product.

---

## 1. Morning brief — 12 minutes

**Trigger:** first open of the day.

Signs in → Today. Header states the date, when they last visited, how many developments
were selected and the estimated minutes against their budget.

Reads the three that matter. One is a partnership announcement; the badges say
`Announced`, `Company self-reporting`, `Self-reported only`. They know before reading
the headline that this is intent, not outcome.

Taps "Why am I seeing this?" on an item they doubt: *"It is in fashion-apparel, which you
follow. It involves a company on your watchlist. Only the company itself has reported
this so far."* The last line is the one that changes how they read it.

The adjacent signal is a regulatory development outside their industries. They would not
have searched for it.

Reaches the bottom, marks the brief done: **"You are caught up."** Nothing more loads.
They choose to leave.

**Verified:** brief composition, budget enforcement, badges, ranking reasons, caught-up
state.

## 2. Following a thread to its evidence

**Trigger:** a headline that matters.

Opens the insight. Verified facts are verbatim sentences with a green rule. Clicks
*View evidence [1]* → the evidence page shows the claim, the exact character range, and
that range highlighted inside the surrounding stored text.

Six timestamps: event date, published, source last updated, discovered by us, version
retrieved, claim last verified. The event date says *"date not stated in source"* — which
is more useful than a guess.

Follows the link to the original. Comes back and reads Counter-signals: *"Sources state
different figures: 18% versus 7%."* The 18% figure is now something to ask about rather
than repeat.

**Verified:** evidence navigation, offset-exact highlighting, timestamp separation,
contradiction display.

## 3. Preparing for a client meeting — 5 minutes

**Trigger:** a meeting in an hour.

Prepare → selects the company, states the objective, sets a 90-day lookback.

Gets a 60-second brief, what changed in each window, verified facts with citations, "what
this could mean" under an interpretation label, five specific questions, a contrarian
angle, and known unknowns.

The questions are specific: *"The announcement sets out intent rather than deployment.
Between decision rights and adoption, which is the binding constraint on getting from
signed agreement to something running in production?"* — not "what are your biggest
challenges?"

Copies the brief. Citations travel with it.

**Verified:** brief generation through the evidence-checked Companion path, specific
starters, copy with citations.

## 4. Asking a question they cannot answer

**Trigger:** a colleague asks something specific.

Companion → *Explore it* → *"What is happening with markdown and allocation in retail?"*

Answer: *"2 evidenced statements bear on this. As of 1 September 2026, based on 2
evidenced claims from 2 sources."* Facts numbered and cited; interpretation separate;
unknowns listed.

Then asks something the sources genuinely cannot answer. The Companion refuses:

> I do not have sufficient verified evidence in the monitored sources to answer this
> reliably. Rather than answer from general knowledge, here is what is missing.

That refusal is the most valuable thing in the journey. Everything else the Companion
says is now credible.

**Verified:** claim-level retrieval with a relevance floor, citations, honest refusal.

---

## 5. Being challenged

**Trigger:** they have formed a view.

Companion → *Challenge me* → *"Challenge the claim that AI allocation reduces markdown."*

Names the assumptions — self-report accepted at face value, baseline stability assumed,
stated intent equated with delivery. Offers an alternative explanation. Where no
contradicting evidence exists it says so, and adds that **absence of contradiction is not
confirmation**.

It does not manufacture opposition. An invented counter-argument would be the same
failure as an invented fact.

**Verified:** assumption identification, counter-evidence retrieval, honest absence.

## 6. Building depth over weeks

**Trigger:** an insight referenced a concept they do not fully own.

Follows the learning connection → *"How fashion retailers make money"* at Foundation.
Objective, explanation, structured model, key terms, common misconceptions, practical
questions.

Answers the knowledge check. Gets it wrong. The explanation says why:

> Rising markdown alongside falling full-price sell-through means units are being cleared
> rather than sold at intended price… which points upstream to the buy, not to pricing
> execution.

Concept state moves to `needs_refresh` with a review date. The sidebar shows the reason.

Weeks later, on the Expert unit, "what changed since" lists recent events touching the
same concept. The fundamentals stayed connected to now.

**Verified:** learning connections, three depths, checks that explain, knowledge state
with reasons, spaced resurfacing, event linkage.

---

## 7. Watching a company

**Trigger:** an account is up for renewal.

Explore → the company. Timeline newest first, each entry showing event type, maturity,
evidence strength, and whether only the company has reported it.

The implementation mix says 12 `Announced`, 2 `Pilot`, 1 `Discontinued` — with the note
that counting announcements is not a measure of market leadership.

Source coverage says which sources are monitored, and that a thin timeline means limited
monitoring rather than an inactive company. They know what they do not know.

**Verified:** timelines, maturity mix, coverage honesty.
