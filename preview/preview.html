Fashion & Apparel

Design, production and sale of clothing, footwear and accessories. Distinguished from general retail by short product life cycles, high style risk and long sourcing lead times — the combination that makes fashion an inventory-timing problem more than a selling problem.

6 value chain stages · 5 KPIs · market model reviewed and dated, kept distinct from the news above it

Market structure

A barbell: vertically integrated fast fashion and ultra-fast digital players at one end, luxury houses with pricing power at the other, and a squeezed mid-market between them. Manufacturing is concentrated in Asia with lead times of 8–40 weeks, which is why nearshoring recurs as a strategic theme. Resale and rental are growing but remain small relative to primary sales.

Value chain

Upstream to downstream. Each stage notes where margin actually sits.

1 Design & product development

Trend interpretation, line building, sampling.

Profit pool: Determines style risk. Cheap to change here, ruinous to change later.

2 Merchandising & buying

Translating the range plan into an open-to-buy and committed orders.

Profit pool: The commitment point: capital is locked here months before demand is known.

3 Sourcing & manufacturing

Supplier selection, capacity booking, production, quality.

Profit pool: Cost price and lead time are set here; lead time is the constraint on responsiveness.

4 Distribution

Inbound logistics, distribution centres, market allocation.

Profit pool: Cost pool; speed here partially compensates for long production lead times.

5 Retail & digital sales

Own stores, wholesale, own digital, marketplaces.

Profit pool: Channel mix determines realised margin more than list price does.

6 Resale, repair & recycling

Take-back, resale, repair and material recovery.

Profit pool: Currently a cost of compliance for most; a margin pool for very few.

KPI tree

What moves, and why an executive cares.

Design-to-shelf lead time 3 weeks (ultra-fast) to 12 months (traditional)

Elapsed time from design freeze to product on sale.

Every week removed is a week closer to real demand and a smaller pre-season bet.

Open-to-buy

Budget remaining for future purchases within a plan period.

The financial control that stops merchandising over-committing. Where planning discipline is actually enforced.

Return rate 20–50% for online apparel in Europe

Share of shipped units returned.

In digital fashion, returns decide whether an order was profitable. Fit and content quality drive it more than policy does.

Sell-through rate 70–90% by season end

Share of a buy sold within the season.

The verdict on the buy. Below plan means either the product or the quantity was wrong, and the two need different fixes.

Open questions
  • Does ultra-fast fashion economics survive regulation on textile waste and import de minimis rules?
  • Can traceability be delivered at product level without a step change in supplier data quality?
  • Is AI shortening the design-to-shelf cycle in practice, or only the content production part of it?
